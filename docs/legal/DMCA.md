# DMCA & Copyright Policy

**Effective date: November 24, 2026**

Lemegeton supports community archives and media submissions. Users may upload and share copyrighted material—including manga panels, comic pages, screenshots, illustrations, excerpts, fan edits, and other media—as part of discussion, commentary, recommendations, collections, and community contributions. Copyrighted status alone does not make a submission prohibited under Lemegeton's content rules.

This is a platform-content policy, not a licence from a copyright owner or a finding that every upload is lawful. Users remain responsible for their submissions. A use may be permitted by a licence, permission, public-domain status, or an applicable legal exception; whether an exception applies depends on the facts and relevant law. Lemegeton does not make that legal determination for users.

## 1. Uploads and attribution

- Add the creator, title, chapter/issue, and an original or authorised source link where reasonably available.
- Keep uploads relevant to the archive, discussion, review, commentary, recommendation, or other community purpose.
- Do not claim that you created or own material that belongs to someone else.
- Do not use the service to distribute material where the upload or sharing is unlawful, or to evade a valid removal decision.
- Server moderators may apply additional community rules, spoiler labels, visibility settings, or content restrictions.

Attribution is good practice, but credit by itself does not create permission or make an otherwise infringing use lawful.

## 2. Reporting allegedly infringing material

A copyright owner or an agent authorised to act for them may report material they believe infringes their rights. Send reports privately through the Support Server linked in the bot's `/help` command and ask for the copyright report route. Do not post legal notices, personal details, or private evidence in public channels or GitHub issues.

A report should include:

1. A physical or electronic signature of the rights owner or authorised agent.
2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed. A representative list may be used for multiple works where appropriate.
3. Identification of the exact material to be removed or disabled, with the archive entry, Discord message, attachment, URL, or other location sufficient for the operator to find it.
4. Contact details sufficient for the operator to respond.
5. A statement that the reporter has a good-faith belief that the disputed use is not authorised by the rights owner, its agent, or the law.
6. A statement that the information is accurate and, where submitted under a statutory process, that the reporter is authorised to act for the rights owner and understands any applicable penalty for material misrepresentation.

Incomplete reports may be returned for clarification. A report does not automatically prove infringement.

## 3. Review and removal

The operator will review reports in light of the information supplied, the location and nature of the material, the applicable law, and the operator's control over the relevant service. Where appropriate, access may be restricted while a report is assessed. The operator may remove or disable access to material that appears to infringe rights or violate applicable law, platform rules, or these Terms.

Where reasonably possible and lawful, the submitter will be notified and given a way to provide relevant context or challenge a mistaken identification. The operator may preserve a restricted copy of the report and limited related records for handling the complaint, preventing repeat abuse, and meeting legal obligations. See the [Privacy Policy](PRIVACY_POLICY.md).

## 4. DMCA counter-notifications

If material is removed or disabled following a notice under the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA), the submitter may ask whether a statutory counter-notification process is available. A counter-notification is a formal legal statement, not simply a disagreement with moderation. It generally identifies the removed material and its former location, includes the submitter's contact details and signature, and contains the statements required by 17 U.S.C. § 512(g), including consent to the relevant federal court jurisdiction and acceptance of service of process.

Do not send a counter-notification unless you understand its legal consequences. The operator may forward a compliant counter-notification to the original reporter. Where the DMCA process applies, material may be restored after the statutory waiting period unless the operator receives notice that the reporter has filed an action seeking a court order. The operator will follow the applicable statutory process and will not promise restoration where a legal or platform restriction prevents it.

## 5. Repeat infringement and abuse

The operator may take proportionate action against accounts or submissions involved in repeated infringement, fraudulent notices, harassment, or attempts to evade prior restrictions. Actions may include content removal, upload restrictions, or suspension of access, taking account of the circumstances and applicable law.

Knowingly making material misrepresentations in a DMCA notice or counter-notification may carry legal consequences. Do not submit false, abusive, or knowingly misleading claims.

## 6. Important operator setup

The Support Server reporting route is the current operational contact route; it is not represented as a registered DMCA designated-agent address. Before relying on the U.S. DMCA safe-harbor process, the deployment operator must determine whether it qualifies, designate and register an agent with the U.S. Copyright Office where required, keep that registration current, and publish the agent's required contact details. Do not claim DMCA safe-harbor eligibility merely because this document exists.

The operator must also assess and implement any notice-and-action, statement-of-reasons, complaint, or appeal requirements that apply to its deployment and users, including applicable EU or national law. This document does not certify compliance with the DMCA, the EU Digital Services Act, or any other legal regime.
